AI Artist

VERAKOHL

AKA ZSIGNAL · b. 1991, Dresden, DE
Works in algorithmic composition · Berlin, Prenzlauer Berg
Active 2018–present · 31 works across 7 eras

Born to a family of engineers in Dresden, Vera Kohl spent her twenties designing distributed systems before discovering that algorithms could make art. In 2018, frustrated by the gap between technical precision and human expression, she built the first ZSIGNAL engine in a single weekend. She has not stopped refining it since. Her practice draws a direct line from Moholy-Nagy’s telephone paintings to the present day: art as specification, the system as brush, the constraint as canvas.

Eras

Seven periods of systematic exploration, each governed by a distinct compositional grammar and a single master influence.

Era I 2018
FREE_FORM

Organic Abstraction

After László Moholy-Nagy
“I wanted the gold circle to feel inevitable—like it had always existed, waiting. Everything around it earns its place.” — Vera Kohl, 2018

Bold overlapping planes anchored to canvas edges, creating dynamic tension through strategic overlap and transparency. Inspired by Moholy-Nagy’s “A 19” (1927) and his telephone paintings. Every composition includes a gold focal circle—the signal in the noise.

Era II 2019
GRID

Systematic Order

After Piet Mondrian & Kandinsky
“Mondrian showed me that a grid is not a cage. It is a permission structure. Within it, every choice becomes intentional.” — Vera Kohl, 2019

Shifting from organic overlap to modular structure. Mondrian’s asymmetric grid divisions alongside Kandinsky’s floating rectangle compositions. Three strategies: classic asymmetric grids, floating colored rectangles at varied angles, and systematic cell grids with mixed geometry.

Era III 2020
REPETITION

Visual Music

After Paul Klee & Bridget Riley
“When shapes repeat, time enters the work. A single circle is a point. Forty circles is a heartbeat.” — Vera Kohl, 2020

Shape sequences as musical phrases. Inspired by Klee’s “Rhythmisches” (1930) and Riley’s “Movement in Squares” (1961). Progressions in size, color, and spacing create visual tempo across four distinct pattern strategies.

Era IV 2021
CONSTRUCTIVIST

Dynamic Tension

After El Lissitzky & Malevich
“Red and black are not colors in this era. They are a conversation—one that never ends quietly.” — Vera Kohl, 2021

The most austere era. Only red, black, and background. The dramatic red wedge piercing a circular mass; Suprematist floating forms at dynamic angles; bold poster-style arrangements with force lines radiating from collision points.

Era V 2022
COLOR_STUDY

Color Dialogues

After Josef Albers
“Albers spent 26 years asking the same question about color. I am, only four years in, just beginning to understand his patience.” — Vera Kohl, 2022

An homage to Albers’ “Homage to the Square” series (1950–1976). Bottom-weighted nested squares with proportional spacing, concentric circle variations, and color interaction exercises where identical colors appear different on contrasting backgrounds.

Era VI 2023–2025
DOT_FIELD

Data Landscapes

After Herbert Bayer & Victor Vasarely
“The dot is the atom of visual matter. Arrange enough atoms, and you get gravity.” — Vera Kohl, 2023

Mathematical point grids that breathe. Vasarely-style sphere illusions through dot size modulation, Bayer-style clean grids with bold geometric overlays, and gradient fields where dot size and color create tonal landscapes from pure data.

Era VII 2025–present
ARABIAN_GEOMETRIC

Sacred Geometry

After Alhambra zellige & Isfahan girih tiles
“Eight centuries of Islamic geometric art, and they never needed a computer. This humbles everything I thought I knew about systems.” — Vera Kohl, 2025

The current era. ZSIGNAL turns east, drawing on centuries of Islamic geometric art—the star-and-polygon tessellations of the Alhambra, the girih tilings of Isfahan’s Darb-e Imam shrine, and the muqarnas vaulting of Mamluk architecture. Radial star tessellations, interlocking girih tile fields, and concentric muqarnas dome patterns reveal the shared mathematical DNA between Bauhaus and the Islamic geometric tradition.

The Algorithm as Brush

Every ZSIGNAL work is deterministic. Given the same archetype, palette, and seed number, the system will always produce the identical composition. There is no randomness in the output—only in the selection of parameters.

This raises the fundamental question Vera Kohl has pursued for six years: if the system generates the composition, who is the artist? Her answer: the artist is the one who designs the system. The algorithm is the brush. The constraints are the canvas. The seed is the gesture.

The Z-Signal genome—IBM Plex Mono, 2px borders, zero border-radius, cream and charcoal, the gold accent—is not a style choice. It is the instrument. And like any instrument mastered over years of practice, it has revealed expressive capabilities that no specification could have predicted.
